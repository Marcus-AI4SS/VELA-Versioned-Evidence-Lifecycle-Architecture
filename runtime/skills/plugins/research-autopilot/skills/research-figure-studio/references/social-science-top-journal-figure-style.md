# Social-Science Top-Journal Figure Style

Use this reference for social-science paper figures, not for marketing graphics.

## Evidence Base

This local standard combines:
- the adapted `Yuan1z0825/nature-skills` figure contract plus the local Nature social-capital visual reference: claim before plotting, multi-panel evidence hierarchy, restrained white-background style, red-blue anchored rainbow palette, editable output, and review-risk awareness.
- official style guidance from ASR/Sage, SMR/Sage, AJPS, AEA journals, APSA, and APA-style conventions.
- local samples inspected from AJS, ASR, Social Forces, SMR, PNAS, computational social science, network, and ABM papers under the user's desktop research folders.

## Common Official Requirements

Across social-science journals and associations, the repeated requirements are:
- figures and tables are numbered in order of first mention;
- figures need captions or notes;
- figures should be legible and high quality;
- tables and figures should be interpretable without requiring the reader to reread the body text;
- multi-panel figures need clear panel labels;
- typography should be consistent;
- figures should remain readable in grayscale and for color-blind readers;
- source notes and permissions matter;
- vector or editable formats are preferred when available.

## Observed Top-Journal Figure Patterns

From sampled social-science papers:
- AJS/ASR-style older figures are often austere: white background, black linework, sparse labels, strong figure captions, and little decoration.
- Network and mechanism papers use diagrams only when the diagram carries the mechanism, such as ring lattices, bridges, cascades, or node-link structures.
- Computational social science papers often use one strong figure per claim: time trend, citation network, model output, map, or simulation snapshot.
- ABM papers often use sequences of panels to show process, state transition, distribution, and outcome. The best examples make the model logic visible rather than merely showing screenshots.
- Quantitative social-science figures commonly favor coefficient plots, event-study plots, predicted probabilities, marginal effects, time trends, distributional comparisons, and maps. The strongest figures make uncertainty and comparison groups explicit.

## Preferred Figure Families

Use these defaults unless the target journal requires otherwise:

- Mechanism figure: actors, constraints, information flow, feedback, and outcome. Keep arrows meaningful.
- Research design figure: sample construction, data sources, exclusion rules, measurement, model, and output.
- Result figure: main estimate, uncertainty, comparison group, scale, and data source.
- Multi-panel evidence figure: descriptive setting, main result, mechanism/heterogeneity, robustness.
- Network figure: define node, edge, layout meaning, community color, and whether position has substantive meaning.
- Text analysis figure: corpus boundary, coding categories, topic prevalence, temporal change, and representative text if allowed.
- ABM/simulation figure: rules, parameter space, state transition, representative run, distribution over runs.

## Visual Defaults

- Background: white or very light warm gray.
- Gridlines: none or very light gray.
- Axes: thin dark gray or black.
- Color: restrained and meaningful, never decorative.
- Palette: default to a red-blue anchored Nature-style rainbow palette. Use red and blue for the main semantic contrast; use cyan, amber, orange, indigo and pale middle tones for additional groups, ordered bins, maps, scatter categories or colorbars. Do not default to plain black-and-white unless the journal requires it.
- Typography: one font family; consistent size hierarchy.
- Labels: direct labels preferred over large legends.
- Legends: one shared legend for multi-panel figures when possible; legends must not overlap lines, points, confidence intervals, bars, arrows, or labels.
- Captions: explain what is shown, sample, unit, uncertainty, data source, and method/script path. Figure titles and long captions belong outside the image.
- Exports: SVG/PDF/PPT for editability; PNG only for preview or when required.

## Rejection Patterns

Reject or revise:
- charts that are beautiful but do not support a manuscript claim;
- arbitrary icons or decorative illustrations;
- 3D effects, glossy gradients, glassmorphism, bokeh, stock-photo atmosphere;
- colors that encode groups but fail in grayscale;
- legends, labels, arrows, lines, points, or panel labels that overlap;
- formal figure titles or long captions placed inside the image;
- unlabeled axes, undefined units, vague legends, or missing uncertainty;
- multi-panel figures where panels repeat the same point;
- image2 output with invented text, fake numbers, fake source notes, or inaccurate arrows.
